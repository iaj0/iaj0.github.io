01

Go-Switch Sleeve

Industrial instrumentation · design & manufacturing

SolidWorks3D PrintingDFMSensors

A go-switch is a sensor used across many environments; here it warned engineers of pipeline failures. In the harsh conditions where they were mounted, the sensors threw false warnings — wasting time and forcing plant shutdowns.

Working from technical drawings provided by the engineers, I designed and produced these protective sleeves. Their implementation drastically improved the stability of the sensors and, more importantly, increased plant up-time.

02

UW Mars Rover — Vision & Comms

University of Waterloo Robotics Team · software & mechanical

ROS2C++OpenCVPyQtNVIDIA Jetson

On the software team I measured how much data SiK telemetry radios could carry between a laptop and an NVIDIA Jetson using a ROS2 publisher and subscriber.

In parallel I integrated OpenCV and PyQt into a driver GUI with multiple camera angles: video is published from the Jetson on the rover, sent over comms, and displayed as a live feed by a ROS2 subscriber on the receiving laptop.

03

LEGO EV3 Guitar-Playing Robot

First-semester cornerstone project · Waterloo

LEGO EV3Mechanism DesignCamshaftColor Sensing

My final project for my first semester at Waterloo, built with teammates: a robot made from LEGO EV3 and Tetrix parts that plays a one-string guitar. It can play ten different notes using a camshaft system.

Colour sensors read songs printed on paper as coloured squares — arranged in pairs, one square for timing and one for the note. In our demo, we managed to play Seven Nation Army and Eye of the Tiger.

04

Toyota Innovation Challenge

Hackathon · computer vision

PythonOpenCVHough Transform

Completed during my first term at Waterloo, this hackathon asked us to write a Python OpenCV program that continuously reads and highlights the wheels of a moving car on a conveyor belt.

The program had to end after the car reached a certain point on the conveyor, marked with a red line. With the help of Hough-transform functions, we successfully tracked the moving wheels by the end of the event.

Fusion 360CAMCNCG&M Code

06

Project Murph — Original Quadruped

Original design · in progress

Inverse KinematicsLIDARComputer VisionRaspberry PiFusion 360

An original quadruped robot designed to autonomously measure the number of steps and the elevation of a staircase in an urban setting — data used to judge how accessible it is for disabled people.

Movement is driven by inverse kinematics; LIDAR and ultrasonic sensors gather data while computer vision handles object detection and navigation. Core hardware: Raspberry Pi 4, Arduino Mega, a PWM motor controller, a Pi camera, and ten servos.

07

FIRST Tech Challenge Robot

Team 9202, “Her Majesty’s Engineers” · 1st Place Design Award

Fusion 360CADMechanism DesignFTC

This assembly helped Team 9202, Her Majesty’s Engineers, win the 1st Place Design Award at our tournament in Alexandria. It impressed the judges and established a permanent database of parts and CAD files for future members through Fusion 360’s cloud-based sharing.

Completed by myself over a four-month period, the model includes omnidirectional wheels, two REV hub controllers, a pulley crane, DC motors, continuous servos, and more.
